# v1.5.0 — Deep Research

## One question, one cited report

Some questions don't fit in a chat turn. "Which of these five vendors actually ships SOC 2 Type II, and what do their customers say six months in?" is an afternoon of tab-opening, not a reply. Deep Research is Pie doing that afternoon for you.

You give it a question — and optionally a line about what to emphasize or skip — and it goes away for about ten minutes. First it breaks the question into directions worth searching separately. Then it reads: dozens of sources, following the gaps it finds rather than a fixed list. Then it writes a report where every claim carries a numbered citation, with the full source list at the end.

The whole thing runs on our servers, not in your browser. You can close the side panel, close the tab, go do something else. When it's done Pie sends you a notification; clicking it opens the report.

## Watching it work

The research page has a timeline that fills in as the run goes. You see the sub-questions it decided on, which one it's on now, how many sources each has turned up, and a running list of what it just read. If a direction turns out to be a dead end it says so rather than quietly dropping it.

A finished report can go two places: **Send to chat** drops it into the composer as a draft so you can keep working with it, and **Download .md** saves it as a file. Reports come back in your interface language.

## Getting to it

There's a **Research** entry alongside chat and settings in the side panel. The composer also has a **Deep research** shortcut — hit it and whatever you'd typed comes along with you.

Deep Research is part of Pie Membership: three runs a week, every model included. One run at a time; you can cancel a run you no longer want. If a run fails on our side it doesn't count against your week.

Not subscribed? The Research page shows three real reports we ran, end to end, so you can read the actual output before deciding.

## Also in this release

**Tools your model ran on the server now show up.** When a provider executes a tool on its own side — OpenRouter's web search, a gateway's MCP call — Pie used to trip over the result. Those now render as read-only remote steps in the sidebar, marked with a ↗, instead of being pushed back through Pie's own tool loop and failing.

**Pie Link updates itself completely.** The menu bar app used to be frozen at whatever version your installer put there, even after the background service updated itself. Both halves now update together — no more reinstalling a `.pkg` to catch up.

Smaller fixes: research timestamps show correctly in the list, the completion notification is translated, and only real `http(s)` links are turned into clickable citations.
